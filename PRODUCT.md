# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Developers and small engineering teams using Anvil as mission control for repository-aware work. They move between chat, code, reviews, automation, and delivery, often while several agents or tools are active. They need to understand what is happening, intervene when useful, and reach the underlying evidence without leaving the app.

## Product Purpose

Anvil coordinates AI-assisted development work across local repositories and connected services. It should make complex work visible and controllable: users can define intent, choose how work runs, inspect progress, open the real threads and artifacts, and reuse proven workflows across workspaces.

## Brand Personality

Technically sharp, pragmatic, and quietly playful. Anvil should feel capable and direct, with helpful gremlin energy that never gets in the way of serious work.

## Anti-references

Avoid corporate dashboard theatre, generic SaaS card grids, ornamental automation bubbles, and workflow canvases that look impressive while hiding execution truth. Do not imitate Zapier-style diagrams when the graph has richer state, context, and evidence.

## Design Principles

1. Show the real work. Progress, decisions, threads, and failures should be inspectable rather than summarized into vague status chrome.
2. Complexity earns progressive disclosure. Keep the main task legible, then let users inspect or steer details without losing their place.
3. Familiar controls, distinctive moments. Use established desktop and canvas mechanics, saving personality for drag, connection, completion, and recovery moments.
4. Templates are portable, runs are contextual. Reusable workflow definitions live above workspaces; executions bind to a workspace and preserve their evidence.
5. The user remains in command. Automation must support pause, retry, explanation, and navigation into the underlying thread.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, complete keyboard navigation, visible focus states, non-colour status cues, screen-reader labels, and reduced-motion behavior. Delight must never delay execution or obscure state.
